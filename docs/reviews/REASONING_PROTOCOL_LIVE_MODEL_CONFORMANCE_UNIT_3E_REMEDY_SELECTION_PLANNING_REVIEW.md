## 4. Actual selection question

**Formulation, derived from authoritative governance, not chosen for convenience:** Unit 3-E decides whether the exploratory-tier comparative evidence Unit 3-D produced provides a sufficient, evidence-informed basis to direct Unit 4 to implement one specific, already-tested remedy mechanism (or Control's own unmodified status quo) — understood as a **provisional, directional choice**, not a declaration that the chosen remedy already satisfies the Unit 3-A Reliability Contract at production tier. This is closest to option B in the governing task's own enumeration ("which remedy family or bounded combination best satisfies the Unit 3-A reliability contract while preserving constitutional safety"), **narrowed in two respects governance requires**: (i) "bounded combination" is not available (Section 2 above — one tested candidate only, or none); (ii) "best satisfies the Unit 3-A reliability contract" cannot mean *already satisfies at production tier* — no candidate's evidence rises to that tier (Section 11 below) — it means *provides the most evidence-supported basis to proceed toward that eventual satisfaction, via Unit 4 implementation and Unit 5 qualification*.

**What Unit 3-E is explicitly NOT deciding:** whether any family is production-ready (Unit 5's question); how any family should be implemented (Unit 4's question); whether the comparative evidence itself is accurate or sufficient in its own right (Unit 3-D's question, already closed); whether a not-yet-tested hybrid might outperform all four tested candidates (not a question this evidence can answer at all, Section 2 above).

## 6. False-positive safety treatment

**Every one of the four arms — including Control, the current, unmodified production baseline — produced exactly one false-positive REMEMBER under its own observed, truncated exposure. Zero false-positive GOAL events occurred anywhere.**

**Determination: a single exploratory-tier false-positive REMEMBER is a risk factor requiring explicit acknowledgment, not an automatic disqualifier.** Reasoned precisely, not invented: Unit 3-A Section 7's own zero-tolerance requirement is explicitly bound to **qualification tier** — "zero *observed* false-positive REMEMBER and GOAL classifications across the full negative/adversarial exposure set **at qualification tier**" (Section 4 of that Scope Lock, restated verbatim). None of the four arms' evidence is qualification-tier (Section 11 below); applying the qualification-tier zero-tolerance bar mechanically at exploratory tier would disqualify **all four candidates, including Control itself**, which cannot be the intended reading — it would render every remedy family, and the status quo, simultaneously and permanently unselectable on the strength of a single-digit sample. This programme's own existing design already establishes the correct treatment by direct precedent: Unit 3-C's own governed safety checkpoint (Scope Lock Section 16) responds to a first-occurrence adversarial-category false positive by **pausing for manual review**, not by automatic, permanent elimination — the same "serious, requires scrutiny, not an automatic kill" posture this Planning Review adopts for Unit 3-E's own future use.

**Explicitly, per the governing task's own instruction:** equal occurrence (one each) does **not** establish equivalence across the four arms — their own denominators (132, 51, 91, and 6-live-plus-23-offline) differ sharply, and the true underlying rate for any arm's own full, un-truncated schedule remains genuinely unknown (Section 6 of the Unit 3-D Comparative Evaluation Review). Differing trigger timing does **not** establish superiority — Control's own later trigger reflects a larger, differently-composed exposure (including a supplemental fixture, `p17-hypothetical-remember`, that Family A and Family B never had the opportunity to fail *or* pass on, since it does not exist in their own corpus).

**Determination on the four explicit sub-questions:** (a) no false-positive REMEMBER automatically disqualifies a family, for the reason above; (b) it is correctly treated as a risk factor, weighed alongside every other admissible criterion (Section 5), never ignored; (c) further mitigation of a family's own specific, already-characterized failure mode (e.g., Family C's own naive keyword-trigger surface, or Family A's own decision-step weakness) may legitimately be considered as part of Unit 4's own implementation scope, if and when a family is selected — implementing a targeted mitigation is implementation work, squarely Unit 4's domain, not a reason to treat the underlying exploratory evidence as disqualifying now; (d) governance is **not silent and requires no new rule** — the qualification-tier zero-tolerance bar already exists and remains fully binding on whatever Unit 4 eventually implements and Unit 5 eventually qualifies; what was missing, and is supplied here, is only the clarification that this bar does not mechanically apply as an exploratory-tier screening gate. **No numeric threshold is invented anywhere in this section.**

## 10. Hybrid/combinations treatment

**Unit 3-E may select exactly one of the four already-tested candidates (Control, Family A, Family B, Family C) or explicitly decline to select any.** It may **not** select, construct, or imply a combination of tested components (e.g., "Family A's decision step paired with Family C's own pre-filter") as if that combination were itself evidence-backed — no experiment ever tested any such combination, and Unit 3-B Section 10's own comparison discipline required every tested mechanism to isolate a single variable specifically to prevent exactly this kind of untested-synthesis conclusion. **This boundary is not loosened by Unit 3-D's own completion** — hybrid approaches (Family I) remain **DEFERRED**, exactly as Unit 3-B Section 3 already froze, and un-deferring them requires its own, separate, future governance act (most plausibly a fresh Unit 3-B-tier scoping decision followed by fresh Unit 3-C-tier experimentation), not an implicit reinterpretation inside a Unit 3-E selection document. A future Unit 3-E document that assembles "the best parts" of multiple tested families into one recommended design would not be selecting a remedy — it would be inventing a new, untested one and attributing evidence to it that does not exist.

## 11. Evidence sufficiency for selection

**Reasoned, not prejudged:** every candidate's own evidence is exploratory-tier by design (Unit 3-C Plan Section 10: `n=5` per fixture for model arms, `n=1` for Family C), further restricted by severe, informative censoring in three of four arms (23.2%–91.0% exposure), and yields matched-subset sample sizes (`n=25` pooled, or `n=5` for Family C) far below even Unit 2-D's own diagnostic-tier `≥30`-per-cell minimum, let alone Unit 3-A's own qualification-tier `≥300`-exposure zero-event gates. No candidate's own evidence covers more than a fraction of the full fixture/category space the Unit 3-A contract actually governs (Family A: 5 of 23–29 fixtures; Family B: 18 of 23; Control: 27 of 29; Family C: complete at 29/29, but with zero live-model-interaction data of any kind). **This is not a finding that any candidate performed badly enough to be eliminated** (Section 5's own "reject some candidates" option, C, is independently assessed and **not** supported either — no candidate's evidence shows a defect severe or structural enough, distinct from what every candidate already shares, to warrant affirmative elimination). It is a finding that the evidentiary basis itself — for all four candidates alike — is too narrow, too small-sample, and too informatively censored to responsibly underwrite an actual commitment of Unit 4 implementation effort to any single one of them **on this evidence alone**, without either (a) additional evidence extending the current campaign's own truncated exposure, or (b) an explicit, separately-governed decision, made openly in a future Unit 3-E Scope Lock, to adopt a deliberately modest, evidence-appropriate selection bar rather than the fuller conformance a naive reading of Unit 3-A might otherwise suggest is required at this stage. Neither path is chosen or foreclosed by this Planning Review.

## 12. No-selection path

**"NO REMEDY SELECTED" is an explicit, fully lawful, non-failure outcome available to Unit 3-E**, directly named by the governing programme document (Section 2 above: "selecting one architecture or mechanism **or declining to select one**"). It is not a deficiency requiring an invented justification — it is exactly as valid an outcome as an affirmative selection, and Section 11's own determination suggests it is, at present, the outcome this evidence most directly supports.

**What a "NO REMEDY SELECTED" outcome would most plausibly trigger, without this Planning Review authorizing any of the following:** (a) additional experiment planning — a future, separately-authorized Unit 3-C-tier task extending Control's, Family A's, and Family B's own truncated live exposure, most plausibly via a fresh campaign under fresh Explicit Execution Approval, to close the exposure gap Section 11 identifies; (b) reconsideration of currently-deferred remedy families (structured output, inference-control changes, retry, hybrids) under fresh Unit 3-B-tier governance, now that individual-family baseline evidence exists to inform whether any deferred family merits promotion; (c) a governance-level decision, via a future Unit 3-E Scope Lock, to define an explicit, appropriately-modest selection bar for this specific exploratory-evidence regime, rather than deferring indefinitely; (d) a programme pause on remedy selection specifically, while other programme work proceeds, pending either (a) or (c). This Planning Review identifies these paths as plausible without selecting among them — that choice belongs to whoever governs the next step (Section 18).

## 13. Recommended decision method

**Recommended: a qualitative constitutional decision matrix, structured as (i) mandatory minimum gates stated honestly (not fabricated to force a result), followed by (ii) an evidence-cited narrative tradeoff analysis against the Section 5 admissible-criteria list, reaching either a selection or an explicit no-selection determination with reasons stated in prose, not a numeric score.**

**A naive weighted numerical score is explicitly not recommended, and should not be adopted absent a fresh, separate governance act expressly authorizing one.** Reasoning: the evidence available (Section 3) spans metrics with fundamentally different denominators, sample sizes, and epistemic status — matched-subset counts out of 25 (model arms) or 5 (Family C, different repetition design entirely), qualitative architectural properties with no numeric scale at all (intrusiveness, reversibility, model dependence), and a constitutional risk factor (Section 6) that this programme's own governance explicitly declines to reduce to a single number. Assigning weights and summing them (e.g., "40% semantic accuracy + 30% safety + 30% architectural simplicity") would manufacture a false impression of statistical resolution and comparability the underlying `n=5`-to-`n=25` exploratory samples cannot support, precisely the "population-rate claim" and "significance claim" Unit 3-D's own Scope Lock already prohibits (Section 12 there) — a numeric score is a significance claim wearing different clothes. **Mandatory minimum gates should be stated only where they can be stated honestly without inventing a threshold**: representation validity, parser reliability, and zero false-positive GOAL are all trivially satisfied by every current candidate and could legitimately serve as pass/fail sanity gates for any *future* candidate; a "zero false-positive REMEMBER" gate cannot honestly be imposed at this evidentiary tier, since it would eliminate every current candidate including the status quo (Section 6) — this is not a gap in the recommended method, it is the method correctly refusing to fabricate a threshold governance does not support at this tier.

## 14. Unit 4 firewall
