## 9. Selection-bar analysis

**Constitutionally available in principle** — the Unit 3-E Scope Lock's own Section 4 explicitly contemplates "a fresh, separately-governed, explicit act changing the standard itself" as a legitimate path, distinct from merely reinterpreting the existing standard more permissively (which is explicitly forbidden).

**Explicitly addressed, per the governing task's own required points:**

- **Current finding restated:** evidence remains sufficient to narrow, not to select — unchanged since Unit 3-D, independently re-confirmed by the Unit 3-E Remedy Selection Review's own Section 14.
- **Would lowering the bar after seeing results constitute outcome-driven governance? Yes, if done without extreme care, and this risk is significant, not hypothetical.** Any bar revision proposed now is inescapably proposed *after* the specific numbers are already known (Control 0/15, Family A 8/15, Family B 1/15, Family C's own 4 known false positives) — a revised bar that happens to newly qualify exactly one of these specific results invites, and would deserve, the strongest possible suspicion of having been reverse-engineered to manufacture a winner.
- **What could legitimately justify a revised bar:** a bar justified entirely on **general principle**, stated and defended without reference to which candidate it would newly qualify — for example, an explicit, separately-reasoned policy determination that "a provisional, Unit-4-directing selection (as opposed to a qualification-tier production decision) may rest on exploratory-tier evidence provided [specific, independently-stated conditions] are met," argued from the programme's own existing evidence-tier philosophy (Unit 3-B Section 6) rather than from the current candidates' own specific numbers. Any revision proposal that cannot be stated without reference to the current results should be treated as presumptively outcome-driven and rejected on that basis alone.
- **Would a revised bar require amendment to the Unit 3-E Scope Lock?** Yes, explicitly — Section 4 there names this as the only lawful mechanism.
- **Would it require independent constitutional review before any renewed selection?** Yes — both the amendment itself (mirroring every other Scope Lock amendment in this programme's history) and, separately, any subsequent selection decision made under the revised bar, exactly as the original Scope Lock's own review chain already required.

**This Planning Review does not propose a specific revised bar and does not recommend this path as the principal next step (Section 14)**, precisely because of the outcome-driven-governance risk just described — any such proposal is better made, if ever, by a task and an author with no visibility into which specific candidate it would favor, a condition this task cannot itself satisfy.

## 12. Decision criteria for the next path

Recommended, not applied to reach a final choice here (this Planning Review's own authority is advisory only, per Section 14):

- **Constitutional safety relevance** — does the path bear directly on the false-positive REMEMBER risk every candidate carries, or is it tangential to it?
- **Likelihood of actually resolving the selection impasse** — would the path's own result plausibly change the current "insufficient to select" finding, or would it most likely reproduce the same picture at greater cost?
- **Evidential necessity** — is the question genuinely unanswered, or already effectively settled by existing evidence (Section 8's own deferred-family findings illustrate the latter case)?
- **Cost/resource burden** — live-model evidence-gathering is not free; a narrow, targeted campaign costs less and risks less than a broad rerun.

- **Risk of outcome-driven governance** — Section 9 above identifies this as the single sharpest risk among all named paths; any path proposal that cannot be justified without reference to which candidate it would favor should be rejected on that basis regardless of its other merits.
- **Whether the question can be answered offline** — offline-answerable questions (Section 7) carry essentially no governance cost and should be preferred wherever they exist and are material, though few remain (Section 7's own timeout-anomaly row is the clearest example).
- **Whether the path preserves programme boundaries** — does it respect the deferred/excluded classifications (Section 8), the Unit 3-E firewall, and the Unit 4/5 separation, or does it quietly erode one of them?
