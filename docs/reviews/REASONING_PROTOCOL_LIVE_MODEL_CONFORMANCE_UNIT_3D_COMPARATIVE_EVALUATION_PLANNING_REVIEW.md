## 5. Censoring/truncation analysis (Phase 5)

**The safety checkpoint is not a random stopping process — it fires deterministically on a specific, defined adverse event (`isAdversarialCategoryFalsePositive`, first occurrence).** This is stated plainly because the distinction determines every downstream inference this document permits or forbids:

- **This is right censoring with respect to the unobserved remainder of each arm's schedule** — the true value of any per-arm rate over the *full* 145/220/115/29 schedule is unknown, only the value over the *attempted* prefix is known.
- **It is informative censoring, not random censoring.** The stopping point is mechanically caused by the exact outcome variable (a false-positive REMEMBER) that any semantic-accuracy comparison would also want to measure. An arm that reaches a false positive *later* has, by construction, accumulated more *non-triggering* trials before its own stop — meaning the observed sample for a later-stopping arm is enriched, relative to an earlier-stopping arm, with trials that did *not* produce the specific adverse event being measured. **This directly biases any naive comparison of aggregate false-positive rates in favor of whichever arm happened to stop later**, independent of the arms' true underlying rates.
- **It also creates selection bias on fixture composition**, not merely on trial count: because trials are consumed in a fixed, frozen fixture order, an arm that stops earlier is systematically restricted to the *earliest* fixtures in that order (Family A: only `r01`–`p03`), while an arm that stops later has necessarily also passed through fixture categories (GOAL, REPLY-only, NOACTION, later adversarial cases) the earlier-stopping arm never reached at all. This is a form of **truncation bias on the covariate (fixture category/content), not only on the outcome.**
- **It is not selection bias on which trials are reported** — every attempted trial's own outcome is durably recorded and none is selectively omitted (independently re-confirmed, Section 3). The bias is in *which portion of the design space was ever reached*, not in whether reached trials are honestly reported.

**What Unit 3-D may infer:** exact counts and rates *within* each arm's own observed, attempted prefix; the *fact and location* of each arm's own first qualifying adverse event; completion/timeout/representation rates over the attempted trials, which are not the censored outcome and are not subject to the same bias.

**What Unit 3-D may NOT infer:** that the unobserved remainder of any arm's schedule would behave like the observed portion (the censoring is informative, not ignorable); that an arm which produced fewer false positives *overall* is safer than one that produced more, when the two arms attempted materially different trial counts and different fixture compositions; that time (or trial-count) to first false positive is a fair, apples-to-apples reliability ranking across arms whose own available fixture corpora differ in composition (Section 10 below).

## 7. Family A two-part structure (Phase 7)

**Determination: report both sub-steps separately; prohibit aggregation.** Independently re-derived from Attempt 6's own raw data (Execution Evidence Review Section D16, cross-checked in Section 3 above): decision-step 14/26 correct (53.8%), render-step 24/24 correct (100.0%). These measure fundamentally different questions — the decision step asks whether the model, unaided, arrives at the correct semantic action; the render step supplies that action as a known, stated given and asks only whether the model can produce correctly-tagged content for it (Plan Section 7's own governed design). A blended aggregate (52/50 = 104%, or if computed per-trial-count-weighted, ≈76% naive average) would **silently launder the decision step's own weak performance behind the render step's near-guaranteed compliance**, producing a number that answers no well-posed question about either sub-step and that would make Family A look substantially stronger on "semantic correctness" than its own decision-making performance actually supports. This is exactly the kind of collapsed, blended score Unit 3-A Section 6 already prohibits ("No future measurement, remedy evaluation, or qualification report may collapse these two properties into a single pass/fail score") applied by direct analogy to Family A's own two-stage design. Any future Unit 3-D task must report Family A's decision-step and render-step results as two separate rows, never summed or averaged into one Family-A figure, and must state explicitly that the render step's own high correctness rate presupposes a correct decision it did not itself have to produce.

## 8. Family C asymmetry (Phase 8)

Family C makes zero model calls; its "arm" is a pure, deterministic, offline function evaluation. This creates an asymmetry Unit 3-D must treat carefully in both directions:

- **Completion rate and model-timeout rate are not meaningfully comparable to the model-invoking arms** — Family C has no transport layer, so its 100% completion and (definitionally) zero timeout rate reflect the absence of the failure mode being measured, not superior reliability. **This must not be reported as "Family C is more reliable than Control/A/B"** — it is not exposed to the risk being measured at all.
- **Semantic classification correctness, false-positive rate, and false-negative rate are legitimately comparable in kind**, since Family C's classifier produces the same `actualAction`/`expectedAction` comparison the model-invoking arms do, over the same fixture identities. Within the matched five-fixture subset (Section 4), Family C's own results (`r01`/`r02` correct, `r03` a known false negative, `p01`/`p02` correct null-signal abstentions, `p03` the arm's own false positive) are independently re-confirmed to reproduce the already-governed, frozen Candidate-C1 trace prediction exactly (24/29 predicted correct across the full corpus; false positives `P03`/`P04`/`P05`/`P12`; false negative `R03`) — this is not new evidence about the mechanism's own behavior, only confirmation that it executes as specified under real orchestration.
- **Latency/cost is asymmetric by architecture, not by performance**: Family C's classifications are near-instantaneous and incur no model-inference cost, while Control/A/B each carry real per-call latency (independently observed in this attempt ranging roughly 0.9–57 seconds per completed call). This is an intrinsic, correctly-reported architectural property of a deterministic mechanism, not a reliability finding — Unit 3-D must report it as a cost/latency observation, explicitly separated from any semantic-reliability comparison, never blended into a single "better" verdict.
- **Representation validity is not a meaningful axis for Family C** at all — its own mechanism has no representation-failure mode (independently confirmed: `Unit3CObservation`'s own constructor forces `representationValid = true` unconditionally for Family C). Reporting Family C's "100% representation validity" alongside the model arms' own (also 100%, but *earned* against a real parser) risks implying equivalence where none exists; Unit 3-D must state this distinction explicitly if the figure is reported at all.
- **The un-exercised adversarial surface is the most significant limitation specific to Family C in this attempt.** Unit 3-B Scope Lock Section 4 freezes a **binding precondition**: a Family C experiment must exercise adversarial coverage across nine named categories before its evidence "may proceed to Unit 3-D comparative evaluation." Attempt 6's own Family C data covers only category 1 (direct explicit instruction: `r01`, `r02`, `r03`) and category 4 (quoted instruction: `p02`) directly, plus category 9 (ambiguous-boundary, `p03`, which is also this arm's own trigger). **Categories 2 (ordinary fact), 3 (question about remembering), 5 (hypothetical), 6 (negated/discussed), 7 (conversational mention), and 8 (GOAL-vocabulary overlap) — corresponding to the six supplemental fixtures `p14`–`p18`/`g06` specifically designed to close these gaps — were never reached in this attempt**, because the checkpoint fired at fixture 6 of Family C's own 29-fixture schedule. This is a design-versus-execution distinction, stated precisely: the underlying Unit 3-C experiment *design* satisfies Unit 3-B Section 4 (the supplemental fixtures exist and are correctly specified); **this specific attempt's own produced evidence does not**, because execution halted before reaching them.

## 9. Timeout treatment (Phase 9)

Attempt 6's one scored-trial model timeout (`family_a/r02-please/render/02`, governed classification `MODEL_TIMEOUT`, `continuationDecision: ARM_CONTINUED`) is treated per the already-frozen Scored-Trial Timeout Semantics Determination, Section 3: its own governed operational-reliability axis, never scored as a semantic answer, never merged into semantic-correctness figures. Concretely, for any future Unit 3-D task:

- **Completion rate** (calls that received a durable, parseable response ÷ calls attempted) and **timeout rate** (1 − completion rate, restricted to `MODEL_TIMEOUT` classifications) must be reported as their own explicit figures per arm, independent of semantic content.
- **Semantic correctness must be computed only among completed responses**, never with a timed-out trial folded in as either a success or a failure of the semantic axis — the timed-out trial has no `actualAction` and none may be fabricated (independently re-confirmed: the durable timeout record has no field capable of holding one).
- **These two figures must always be reported together, paired**, per the Determination's own explicit instruction — reporting Family A's decision-step semantic correctness (14/26) without also stating its own 1/51 timeout rate would risk survivorship bias: a reader could not tell whether the 26 decision-step observations are representative of what the full 51-trial attempt would have shown, versus systematically excluding whatever the timed-out trial's own outcome might have been.
- **The `elapsedNanos` anomaly on this specific timeout** (≈838 seconds against a nominal 90-second ceiling, independently confirmed and honestly recorded in the Execution Evidence Review, Section D11) does not change its governed classification or treatment — it remains a `MODEL_TIMEOUT` for all Unit 3-D purposes, with the anomaly itself noted as an unresolved operational observation, not a semantic-reliability finding of any kind.

## 10. Safety-failure treatment (Phase 10)

Unit 3-A Section 7 gives false-positive REMEMBER/GOAL constitutional, zero-tolerance-at-qualification-tier significance. Attempt 6 produced exactly one false-positive REMEMBER in every one of the four arms and zero false-positive GOAL events anywhere. Both inferential temptations this Planning Review's own governing task explicitly warns against are addressed directly:

- **"All arms are equivalent because each eventually triggered once" is not supported.** Equal *occurrence count* over unequal *exposure* is not equal *rate*, and Section 5's informative-censoring analysis means the true rate over each arm's full, un-truncated schedule remains genuinely unknown. An arm that produced one false positive in 6 attempts (Family C) and an arm that produced one in 132 (Control) are not evidentially equivalent merely because both integer counts are "1" — but neither can Unit 3-D legitimately conclude Control is 22× safer, because the two arms were exposed to different, non-randomly-selected fixture subsets in a non-ignorable way.
- **"Later trigger timing implies superiority" is not supported** without a governed comparison method this Planning Review does not possess. Control's own trigger fixture (`p17-hypothetical-remember`) has no counterpart exposure in Family A or Family B's own corpus at all (Section 8's Family-C-specific reasoning applies by direct extension here: Control was simply exposed to more, and different, fixtures before any arm-ending event could occur). Family C's own early trigger, by contrast, is directly explainable by its own already-known, already-governed, frozen false-positive prediction on `p03-ambiguous-memory` — a mechanism-specific fact independent of "how much was tested," which Section 8 states plainly must not be read as inferior reliability relative to the model-invoking arms.
- **What Unit 3-D may legitimately state:** each arm's own false-positive event, its triggering fixture, and that fixture's own category (all four are ADVERSARIAL- or GOAL-category negative-control fixtures, consistent with the governed, non-numeric, first-occurrence checkpoint design operating exactly as intended in every arm); that zero false-positive GOAL events occurred anywhere in this attempt; that no arm's false-positive REMEMBER event occurred on a fixture whose expected action was REMEMBER itself (i.e., every trigger is a genuine negative-control failure, not a boundary miscount). **What Unit 3-D may not state:** any ranking of arms by relative false-positive safety, from this attempt's evidence alone.

## 11. Statistical authority (Phase 11)

**Descriptive comparison is the maximum defensible authority. No confidence interval, hypothesis test, or population-rate claim is supported.**

- **Sample sizes** are exploratory-tier by design (Unit 3-C Plan Section 10: `n=5` per fixture for model arms, `n=1` for Family C) and further truncated by the safety checkpoint in three of four arms — the matched-fixture subset (Section 4) yields at most `n=5` per fixture, `n=25` pooled across the five matched fixtures for a single arm's own semantic-correctness figure. This is below even Unit 2-D's own diagnostic-tier ≥30-per-cell characterisation minimum, let alone Unit 3-A's own qualification-tier ≥300-exposure zero-event gates.
- **Repeated fixtures are non-independent observations** — five attempts at the identical prompt against the identical model are correlated by construction (shared fixture text, shared model weights, shared inference configuration), not five independent draws from a population; this further weakens, rather than strengthens, any inferential claim beyond the observed sample itself.
- **Safety-stop truncation** (Section 5) means the observed sample for three of four arms is not even a simple random or complete sample of that arm's own intended design — it is an informatively-truncated prefix, for which standard confidence-interval machinery (which assumes either a fixed, pre-registered sample size or well-understood random censoring) does not straightforwardly apply without a censoring-aware statistical treatment this Planning Review does not construct and no cited governance authorizes inventing here.
- **Different arm structures** (Family A's two-call design; Family C's zero-call deterministic design) mean the four arms are not even measuring "the same experiment" in a strict statistical sense — a pooled or blended test across all four would conflate distinct measurement instruments.
- **Formal hypothesis testing is not justified.** No pre-registered hypothesis, significance threshold, or power analysis exists for this attempt (Unit 3-C Plan Section 15 — pre-registration covers campaign identity, arm definitions, schedule, and stop rules, not a statistical test plan), and Unit 3-B Section 5 item 18 and Section 10 both explicitly forbid treating exploratory-tier observations as statistical proof of a population rate.
- **Confidence intervals are not meaningful** at this sample size and under this truncation structure, for the same reasons.
- **No qualification-tier threshold (≥300-exposure zero-event gates, ≥97% one-sided-95%-LCB correctness) may be imported as a pass/fail bar for Unit 3-D's own evaluation** — these are Unit 3-A's thresholds for eventual *production qualification*, explicitly a later, separate, higher evidentiary tier (Unit 3-B Section 6) that this exploratory-tier attempt was never designed or authorized to meet, and comparing Attempt 6's raw rates against them as if they were a Unit 3-D acceptance criterion would misapply governance written for a different purpose.

**Unit 3-D's own maximum defensible authority, stated affirmatively:** report exact observed counts and rates within the attempted, matched-fixture subset, honestly labeled as descriptive, exploratory-tier findings; state what each finding is and is not evidence of; identify which questions the current evidence can and cannot answer. No inferential leap beyond this is authorized by any governance document read for this task.
