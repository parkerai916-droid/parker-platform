# Epistemic Integrity — Constitutional Register

This register records the constitutional status and history of Constitutional Amendment No. 1 — Epistemic Integrity. It is a historical and status record only. It does not itself constitute, replace, modify, or reinterpret any constitutional instrument. The binding text is, and remains, `docs/architecture/epistemic-integrity.md`.

---

## Entry: Amendment No. 1 — Epistemic Integrity

* **Amendment Number:** 1
* **Title:** Epistemic Integrity
* **Status:** Ratified
* **Version:** 1.0
* **Ratification Date:** 29 July 2026
* **Articles:** 19
* **Constitutional Tests:** 69
* **Supersedes:** Draft Versions 0.1–0.4 (archived in this directory)
* **Constitutional Scope:** epistemic reasoning, evidence, representation, provenance, evidential integrity, contemporaneity, evidential weight, uncertainty, confidence, revision, and correction

### Review Process

1. Initial drafting (Version 0.1) — established the core architecture: representation rather than absolute truth, evidential states, provenance, contemporaneity, negative evidence, temporal integrity, revision, and correction.
2. Constitutional review and expansion (Version 0.2) — added Propositional Integrity and Integrity of Evidence following a self-critical review that identified missing definitions, an unintegrated contemporaneity passage, and an absent negative-evidence doctrine in the original draft.
3. Constitutional review and narrowed doctrine (Version 0.3) — considered, and adopted in deliberately narrowed form, a Burden of Justification doctrine, after an explicit review rejected most of the originally proposed scope as duplicative of existing articles.
4. Adversarial constitutional audit (of Version 0.3) — an independent, adversarial review that treated the document as a court would before permanent ratification: article-by-article soundness review, internal consistency review, loophole assessment, completeness assessment, layering assessment, test-suite audit, and adversarial red-teaming. The audit concluded the architecture was sound but identified twelve specific findings that had to be resolved before permanent ratification.
5. Narrow hardening amendment (Version 0.4) — resolved all twelve audit findings through targeted textual amendment (materiality reviewability, evidential-state differentiation criteria, an interpretive-hierarchy clause, disclosure of "reasonably practical/available" reliance, a dedicated sufficiency test, a list-exhaustiveness clause, an Article V disclosure duty, an explicit reasoned-conclusion clarification, and three editorial corrections), introducing no new constitutional doctrine.
6. Numbering and cross-reference verification — every article cross-reference and every constitutional test number was independently re-verified after each round of drafting, confirming 19 sequential articles and 69 sequential tests with no gaps or duplicates.
7. Final ratification recommendation — recorded in the Version 0.4 source (`EPISTEMIC_INTEGRITY_V0_4_RATIFICATION_SOURCE.md`), concluding that no constitutional defect remained and recommending ratification as Version 1.0.

### Ratification Basis

* All twelve constitutional audit findings were resolved.
* No remaining constitutional defect was identified at the time of ratification.
* No new constitutional doctrine was introduced in the final drafting cycle; all Version 0.4 changes were disclosure requirements, clarifications, or wording corrections to already-established articles.
* 19 articles were verified, sequentially numbered, with all cross-references checked.
* 69 constitutional tests were verified, sequentially numbered, with no gaps or duplicate test numbers.

### Future Amendment Requirement

* The ratified text (`docs/architecture/epistemic-integrity.md`) is constitutionally frozen.
* Any future change requires a formal constitutional amendment, following a review process of the kind recorded above — not ordinary documentation editing.
* A future amendment should itself be logged as a new entry in this register and as a new milestone in `docs/architecture/ARCHITECTURE_HISTORY.md`, preserving rather than overwriting this entry, consistent with the Temporal Integrity and Revision of Knowledge articles the ratified instrument itself establishes (Articles XVI and XVII).
