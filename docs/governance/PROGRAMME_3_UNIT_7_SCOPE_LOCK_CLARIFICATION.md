# Programme 3 Unit 7 Scope Lock Clarification

Programme: **Programme 3 — Knowledge Memory, Unit 7 Scope Lock Clarification.**

## 1. Status and Authority

This document is subordinate to `docs/architecture/parker-constitution.md` and `docs/architecture/epistemic-integrity.md`, both of which it interprets and applies but does not amend. It is subordinate to `docs/governance/PROGRAMME_3_KNOWLEDGE_MEMORY_CONTRACT_DESIGN_V2.md` (frozen) and `docs/governance/PROGRAMME_3_KNOWLEDGE_MEMORY_SCOPE_LOCK.md` (binding), neither of which it redefines. It does not reopen, narrow, or reinterpret `docs/governance/PROGRAMME_3_UNIT_6_SCOPE_LOCK_CLARIFICATION.md`, which governs a different, closed unit. Per the document hierarchy the Unit 6 Constitutional Reconciliation established (Contract Design V2 → Scope Lock → Implementation Plan → Unit-level Clarification), this document's own authority is the narrowest of the four: it resolves implementation-facing ambiguity Contract Design V2 and the Scope Lock leave open for Unit 7 specifically; it does not, and cannot, override either.

## 2. Purpose

Contract Design V2 §3 and Epistemic Integrity Articles XVI–XVIII establish that Knowledge Memory's promoted items must remain revisable, non-forking, non-erasing, and honestly disclosed over time, and Programme 3's own Scope Lock §8 already resolves the one question it names as open (concurrency) as a non-blocking, implementation-level choice. What remains unresolved, and what this document resolves, is narrower: the concrete shape of `KnowledgeItem.history`, the treatment of already-appended events, the precise relationship between revision and supersession, the disclosed rule for repeated or invalid status transitions, the specific ordering mechanism Scope Lock §8 requires but does not name, whether a lifecycle version value is required, the permission-boundary classification of Unit 7's own acts, and the result/failure vocabulary a lifecycle operation must be able to express. This document resolves each of these and nothing else.

## 3. Constitutional Basis

Epistemic Integrity Article XVI (Temporal Integrity) requires that historical sequence be preserved and that later information never make an earlier conclusion appear better- or worse-supported than it was at the time. Article XVII (Revision of Knowledge) requires that understanding remain revisable and that a refusal to revise in the face of materially stronger evidence is itself a failure. Article XVIII (Correction of Error) requires that the original record be preserved, that the basis of correction be explained, and that a superseded representation never circulate without qualification. Article XV's separation principle (no subsystem determines the final evidential status of its own output) bears on §13's permission-boundary analysis by analogy, exactly as Contract Design V2 §16.9 already applies it to submission-context metadata. The Constitution's "Cognition proposes, Trust authorises, Runtime executes" discipline and its Permission Engine sole-authority statement are the basis for §13's classification, applied here, not reopened.

## 4. Unit 7 Responsibility

Unit 7 is responsible for exactly the lifecycle Contract Design V2 §3 already names for an already-promoted `KnowledgeItem`: revision, supersession (as a form of revision), retirement, and restoration, together with the historical-preservation discipline binding all four. Unit 7 is not responsible for, and does not implement: contradiction handling (unchanged from Unit 6), staleness disclosure at retrieval time (Unit 9), Permission Engine wiring (Unit 8), or any redesign of Unit 6's own promotion evaluation, which this document does not reopen.

## 5. Lifecycle History Contract

`KnowledgeItem.history` must be capable of holding a single, chronologically ordered, non-forking sequence of lifecycle events drawn from a closed set of exactly three variants. `KnowledgePromotion` **is** a lifecycle event and **must not** be duplicated by a separate "revision" type — its own existing shape (`knowledgeId`, `evidenceReference`, `resultingState`, `occurredAt`, `basis`) already, and correctly, covers both an initial promotion and every subsequent revision, exactly as its own KDoc already discloses ("an initial promotion, or a subsequent revision"). Two further variants are authorised, and only two: a retirement event, carrying at minimum `knowledgeId`, `occurredAt`, and a disclosed `basis`, and no `resultingState` (retirement is a status change, never an evidential classification); and a restoration event, carrying at minimum `knowledgeId`, `occurredAt`, a disclosed `basis`, and a reference to the Memory Core evidence that re-establishes support (mirroring `KnowledgePromotion.evidenceReference`'s own already-established shape). No other lifecycle-event variant is authorised by this document. `KnowledgeItem.history`'s element type must widen from `List<KnowledgePromotion>` to a closed, sealed lifecycle-event contract encompassing these three variants; this widening is additive to the type Unit 4 already froze, exercising exactly the latitude Unit 4's own KDoc already reserved to Unit 7 ("that guarantee is a future write-path behaviour (Unit 7's own responsibility)"; "enforcement is Unit 7's own, later, separately authorised responsibility"). This document does not prescribe a Kotlin type name or declaration form; a sealed interface with `KnowledgePromotion` as one existing variant among the two new ones is illustrative, not mandatory.

## 6. Lifecycle Event Immutability

An appended lifecycle event **must not** be edited in place and **must not** be deleted merely because later understanding changes. Any correction to what an earlier event expressed **must** be represented by a new, later, appended event — never by rewriting, replacing, or removing the earlier one. Every previously appended event's classification or status, disclosed basis, timestamp, and evidence/provenance reference **must** remain visible in `history` for the life of the `KnowledgeItem`, without exception. Because `KnowledgeItem` is an immutable value, a lifecycle operation **must** be represented as producing a new `KnowledgeItem` value whose `history` is the prior value's own history with exactly one new event appended, and whose `status` reflects only that new event's effect where applicable; it **must not** be represented as mutating an existing `KnowledgeItem` instance in place. A caller holding a reference to the prior value continues to observe it exactly as it was — this document does not require, and does not authorise, retroactively altering any previously returned `KnowledgeItem` value.

## 7. Revision and Supersession

**Revision Qualification Gate.** A Memory Core reference submitted against an already-promoted `KnowledgeItem` qualifies as a revision only where it bears a structurally disclosed relationship to that item's already-cited evidence. Qualification requires at least one of the following, established through ordinary `MemoryRetrieval.traverseRelationships` (the same mechanism Unit 6 already uses for `SUPPORTS`/`CONTRADICTS`/`DISPUTES`) or through a Memory Core status transition on the already-cited reference itself:

- the new evidence is linked to the existing cited evidence by an `AMENDS` relationship;
- the new evidence is linked to the existing cited evidence by a `SUPERSEDES` relationship;
- the new evidence is linked to the existing cited evidence by a `CONTRADICTS` relationship;
- the new evidence is linked to the existing cited evidence by a `DISPUTES` relationship;
- the same already-cited evidence reference has itself undergone a relevant Memory Core status transition (`MemoryCore.transitionStatus`).

These relationships and transitions establish only that the new evidence materially bears on the existing proposition — satisfying Article XVII's own "materially significant evidence"/"materially stronger evidence" language without requiring a numerical materiality threshold Memory Core cannot supply. They do not themselves dictate the resulting `EvidentialState`: Section 7 fixes only what qualifies an attempt as a revision at all, never what the attempt concludes. Where no qualifying relationship or status transition exists, the submission **is not a revision**: no lifecycle event is appended, the existing `KnowledgeItem` remains entirely unchanged, and the caller receives `NotQualifyingRevision` (Section 15). Such a submission is not thereby prohibited from consideration altogether — it may proceed, where otherwise authorised, as an ordinary new `KnowledgeCandidate` through the Unit 6 promotion path, producing (if promoted) a distinct `KnowledgeItem` with its own `KnowledgeId`, never a revision of the existing one. This document does not permit a caller to revise an existing item merely by association of `KnowledgeId`, and does not introduce any numerical evidence-strength threshold as an alternative gate.

**No-Change Revision Rule.** Qualification to enter revision evaluation (immediately above) and whether that evaluation changes the materialised `EvidentialState` are separate questions. A qualifying revision may lawfully conclude with a changed `EvidentialState`, with an unchanged `EvidentialState` accompanied by a new, disclosed revision event stating that the evidence was reconsidered and the classification did not change, or with any other outcome Section 15 separately authorises (such as a stale-version conflict, which is orthogonal to qualification). Consistent with Article XVII's own bar on treating a well-founded original conclusion's persistence as failure, a qualifying revision **must not** be treated as `NotQualifyingRevision` or any other rejection merely because the classification does not change; it is `Applied`, and its appended event must honestly disclose both the newly considered evidence and the resulting, possibly unchanged, state.

**Supersession.** Supersession qualifies through the `SUPERSEDES` case of the gate above: either a Memory Core `SUPERSEDES` relationship pointing from new evidence to the `KnowledgeItem`'s already-cited evidence, or a `SUPERSEDED` status transition (via `MemoryCore.transitionStatus`) on that already-cited evidence itself — and only through one of these two, precisely stated triggers, never a looser "equivalent relationship" formulation. Supersession **is** an ordinary revision under this gate; it **is not** a distinct lifecycle-event kind and **must not** be given its own event variant beyond the one Section 5 already authorises (`KnowledgePromotion`). A revision **must** create a new `KnowledgePromotion`-shaped event appended to `history`; it **must not** rewrite, replace, or discard the promotion or revision event that preceded it. Arbitrary-length supersession chains **must** remain retrievable, in order, by ordinary traversal of `history` — no separate chain-tracking structure is authorised or required, since `history`'s own ordering already expresses the chain. Unit 7 **must** preserve, on every revision event, the specific Memory Core evidence reference that justified it (mirroring `KnowledgePromotion.evidenceReference`'s existing, per-event field, distinct from `KnowledgeItem.evidenceReference`), so that a later reader can trace exactly which evidence justified which step of the chain. Unit 7 observes both triggers strictly read-only and **must not**, under any circumstance, write a `SUPERSEDED` or any other status, or a `SUPERSEDES` or any other relationship, directly into Memory Core — it only reads Memory Core's own, already-transitioned status or already-created relationship (Memory Core Scope Lock §5: "Knowledge Memory reads Memory Core; it never writes to it"), and this document does not authorise any exception to that boundary.

## 8. Retirement

Retirement **is** a transition of `KnowledgeItemStatus` from `ACTIVE` to `RETIRED`, and **only** that transition. It **must not** delete, remove, or otherwise render inaccessible the `KnowledgeItem`, its prior history, or its provenance. It **must** be accompanied by a disclosed, non-blank basis, mirroring every other disclosed basis in this codebase. It **must** be represented by an appended retirement event (Section 5), never by mutating `status` on an existing value in place (Section 6). **Repeated retirement of an already-retired item must be rejected, not treated as a disclosed no-op and not treated as a new permitted event.** This is the governing rule, chosen because retirement is defined, exhaustively, as the `ACTIVE`→`RETIRED` transition; a request to retire an item already in `RETIRED` status does not name a transition this document defines at all, and accepting it either silently (a no-op) or as a fresh event would fabricate a status change that did not occur or a history entry with no transition behind it. This mirrors Memory Core Scope Lock §8's own governing convention for its sibling lifecycle ("every transition it does not define is rejected"), applied here for the same reason: an undefined transition is refused, not quietly absorbed.

## 9. Restoration

Restoration **is** a transition of `KnowledgeItemStatus` from `RETIRED` to `ACTIVE`, represented **only** by a new, appended restoration event (Section 5) — it **must not** be achieved by erasing, altering, or reversing the retirement event that preceded it, which remains permanently visible in `history` per Section 6.

**Restoration Qualification Rule.** Restoration requires all of the following: (a) the `KnowledgeItem` is currently `RETIRED`; (b) the originally-cited evidence reference underlying the item's retirement resolves through the authorised `MemoryRetrieval` view at the time restoration is attempted; (c) where restoration relies on new supporting evidence distinct from that originally-cited reference, the new evidence reference also resolves through `MemoryRetrieval`; (d) a disclosed, non-blank basis; and (e) where new supporting evidence is offered, a structural relationship showing it materially bears on the retired proposition, established through the same qualifying-relationship family Section 7 defines for revision — `AMENDS`, `SUPERSEDES`, `CONTRADICTS`, or `DISPUTES` between the new supporting evidence and the originally-cited evidence. Where no new supporting evidence is offered, the originally-cited evidence reference's own resolution under (b) is sufficient. No relationship type beyond these four is authorised for this purpose; restoration **must not** rely on any relationship existing governance does not already expressly authorise, and this document does not invent one. A submission that cannot satisfy this rule is not a restoration and must instead proceed, if at all, through a new `KnowledgeCandidate` promotion under Unit 6, never as a revival of the retired item. This safeguard exists so that weak or unrelated evidence cannot bypass Unit 6's own promotion gate merely by being submitted against a dormant `KnowledgeId`.

**Restoration Non-Resolution Rule.** If the originally-cited evidence reference does not resolve through `MemoryRetrieval` at the time restoration is attempted, restoration **is barred**: the existing `KnowledgeItem` **must remain `RETIRED`**, no restoration event may be appended, and any later re-emergence of support for the same proposition **must** enter Knowledge Memory only through a new `KnowledgeCandidate` promotion, producing a new `KnowledgeItem` with its own `KnowledgeId` — never a restoration of the non-resolving item. This document does **not** state or imply that Unit 7 can definitively distinguish permanent owner erasure (Memory Core's `DELETED` status, reserved exclusively to the owner-requested deletion path, Contract Design V2 §3) from every other cause of non-resolution reachable through `MemoryRetrieval`'s own nullable-return contract: neither `MemoryCore.kt` nor the Memory Core Scope Lock guarantees that a `DELETED` record's getter call returns anything distinguishable from a record that fails to resolve for any other reason (a wrong identifier, an as-yet-unbuilt permission-filtering decorator, or any future non-erasure cause). Non-resolution is used here as a conservative safety boundary, not as a claim of fact — Unit 7 does not claim, and must never be implemented to claim, that non-resolution proves owner erasure occurred, and must not fabricate that distinction where the read-only retrieval contract does not support it.

**Attempting to restore an already-`ACTIVE` item must be rejected**, for the identical reason and by the identical governing convention as Section 8's retirement rule: restoration is defined only as `RETIRED`→`ACTIVE`, and an `ACTIVE` item names no such transition to perform.

## 10. Lifecycle Ordering and Concurrency

**The governing rule is a monotonic, per-`KnowledgeItem` lifecycle sequence value, compared against a caller-supplied expected value at the moment of append (compare-and-append).** Every `KnowledgeItem` has exactly one such sequence value, equal to the count of lifecycle events already present in its `history` (the initial promotion is sequence `1`; each subsequent revision, retirement, or restoration event advances it by exactly `1`, regardless of which of the three event kinds it is — one shared sequence, never a separate counter per event kind, since Section 5 fixes `history` as a single sequence, not three parallel ones). A lifecycle operation **must** be evaluated against the sequence value the caller observed when it read the `KnowledgeItem` it intends to act on; where the item's actual current sequence value no longer matches that expectation at the moment of append — meaning some other operation was appended first — the operation **must** be rejected as a stale-version conflict (Section 15), never silently reordered, retried, or applied out of turn. This produces one total order per item and structurally forecloses a forked history: at most one event may ever occupy a given sequence position. Wall-clock timestamps **must not** be used as the ordering key and **must not** be treated as establishing order where they are equal or out of sequence with the append order — the sequence value alone determines order; a timestamp remains a disclosed, honest record of when an event occurred (Section 12), never a substitute for it. This is the smallest rule sufficient for Unit 7: it requires no new identifier type and no external coordination service, and mirrors this codebase's own existing precedent (`InMemoryKnowledgeStore.retrieve`'s own KDoc: "Ordering by structure, not by a potentially-colliding timestamp, is what keeps this a 'fixed, stable, repeatable rule'"). Per Scope Lock §8's own condition, this rule is disclosed here precisely because it is the one, consistently applied mechanism that condition requires; it is not varied by event kind, by code path, or by circumstance.

**Retry and Idempotency.** Because every lifecycle operation is evaluated against the caller-observed expected sequence value and applied only by compare-and-append, a successful append advances the item's lifecycle sequence by exactly `1` and can never be applied a second time against the same prior value: a retry of an already-applied operation, resubmitted with the same, now-stale, expected value, **must** return a stale-version conflict (Section 15), never re-append the same logical event and never silently report success a second time. Two concurrent submissions of equivalent operations against the same prior sequence value can never both succeed — exactly one advances the sequence; the other is rejected as a stale-version conflict. This gives safe retry behaviour without requiring an operation identifier, a deduplication cache, or any distributed-systems coordination machinery; a caller that loses confirmation of a prior attempt need only re-read the item's current state to discover whether its operation already applied. This document does not introduce an operation identifier, since compare-and-append already makes one unnecessary for correctness.

## 11. Lifecycle Version and Identity

A lifecycle version value **is** required, and it **is** the count of events in `history` at any given moment — it **must not** be introduced as a separate, independently stored field, since `history`'s own length already and exclusively determines it. Knowledge Memory owns this value; no other subsystem reads or sets it. It advances by exactly `1` per appended event, of any of the three kinds Section 5 authorises. It is used exclusively to detect a stale expected-version conflict (Section 10); it **does not**, and **must not**, form any part of `KnowledgeItem` identity — `KnowledgeId` alone remains the sole, stable identity of a `KnowledgeItem` throughout every revision, retirement, and restoration it undergoes, exactly as Unit 4 and Unit 6 already established. A lifecycle event's own identity, where one is needed at all, is adequately given by the pair (`KnowledgeId`, its position in `history`); no independent event-identifier type is authorised or required by this document.

## 12. Determinism and Time

`KnowledgeId` generation remains exactly as Unit 6 already established: deterministic, derived from the candidate's own Memory Core evidence reference, unaffected by anything in this document. Lifecycle ordering (Section 10) and the accept/reject outcome of a given lifecycle operation **must** be deterministic for identical authoritative inputs — the same starting `KnowledgeItem` state (including its current sequence value) and the same operation inputs **must** yield the same outcome every time. Each lifecycle event's own `occurredAt` **is** a wall-clock read and **is not**, and **is not required to be**, deterministic — timestamps may legitimately differ across repeated, otherwise-identical operations, following the same disclosed convention Unit 6 already established for `KnowledgePromotion.occurredAt`. **Timestamps alone do not define ordering** (Section 10); an implementation **must not** require identical timestamps across repeated operations as a condition of any test or behaviour this document governs. Retry-safety (Section 10) likewise does not depend on timestamps: a retried or concurrently duplicated operation is distinguished solely by the lifecycle sequence value, never by `occurredAt`, which remains a non-deterministic, honestly disclosed record of wall-clock time only.

## 13. Permission-Boundary Self-Certification

Applying Chapter 10's general admission criteria and CDR-005's Model C domain self-certification authority: revision, supersession-response, retirement, and restoration are each classified as **internal Knowledge Memory computation, not requiring Permission Engine evaluation** — a negative classification, disclosed with reasoning per CDR-005's own symmetric documentation requirement, not merely assumed. The reasoning: each of these four acts operates exclusively on Knowledge Memory's own already-promoted internal records, reacting either to Knowledge Memory's own re-evaluation of Memory Core evidence it already had authorised access to, or to a status change Memory Core itself already performed under its own, separately governed permission boundary; none of the four creates a new external, owner-facing, state-changing effect; none grants, denies, or alters any capability of any module; none exposes anything beyond what Promotion (Unit 6) already exposed without Permission Engine gating. This is the same structural reasoning that classified Promotion as non-gated, applied here to its four lifecycle successors, not a new argument invented for this document.

This classification does not foreclose a future act from carrying an explicit Owner-request as one of its inputs. Where a future amendment introduces such a requirement for any of these four acts, that Owner-request fact **must** be treated exactly as Contract Design V2 §16 treats "explicit request" for promotion: a disclosed, non-evidential, non-authorising submission-context fact, contributing at most to which act is attempted or why — **it must not be treated as, substituted for, or conflated with Permission Engine authorisation**. This document does not itself introduce any such Owner-request input requirement; doing so is outside this document's narrow scope and would require its own disclosed treatment when and if it arises.

## 14. Ownership Boundaries

Knowledge Memory owns the `KnowledgeItem` lifecycle in its entirety — every revision, retirement, and restoration is Knowledge Memory's own act. Memory Core owns evidence and every evidence-status transition (`ACTIVE`, `DISPUTED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `ARCHIVED`, `DELETED`); Unit 7 reads Memory Core's own already-performed transitions and never performs one on Memory Core's behalf. Unit 7 does not implement retrieval-time staleness disclosure, which Contract Design V2 §3 (Amendment 7) reserves to Unit 9. Unit 7 does not introduce Permission Engine wiring of any kind, which remains Unit 8's own, separately governed responsibility. Unit 7 does not redesign, correct, or reinterpret Unit 6's own promotion evaluation, which is closed.

## 15. Result and Failure Semantics

A lifecycle operation **must** be capable of expressing, as a closed, disclosed set of outcomes — mirroring `KnowledgeCandidateEvaluation`'s own sealed, disclosed-basis pattern, never an exception — exactly the following six:

- **Applied.** The operation succeeded: a new lifecycle event was appended and (where applicable) `status` changed. This is the sole outcome for a qualifying revision that considers new evidence and reaches an unchanged classification (Section 7's No-Change Revision Rule) — Article XVI/XVII require every genuine reconsideration to leave a visible trace that it occurred, even where the conclusion did not change, so an unchanged-classification revision is still `Applied`, carrying a disclosed basis stating that the classification was reconsidered and unchanged. There is no separate "no-op" outcome distinct from `Applied` for this case.
- **Stale-version conflict.** The caller's expected lifecycle sequence value (Section 10, Section 11) no longer matches the item's actual current value, whether because another operation intervened or because the caller is retrying an operation of its own that already applied (Section 10, Retry and Idempotency).
- **Structurally unresolvable.** The referenced `KnowledgeItem`, or evidence a revision or restoration cites, cannot be resolved at all through `MemoryRetrieval`, mirroring Unit 6's own structural-prerequisite category exactly. Distinct from `Restoration barred by non-resolution` below, which applies specifically to the originally-cited evidence underlying a retirement.
- **Not permitted from current status.** The requested transition is not one this document defines from the item's current status — repeated retirement of an already-retired item and restoration of an already-active item (Sections 8, 9) are both this outcome, never a silent no-op.
- **Not a qualifying revision (`NotQualifyingRevision`).** The referenced records resolve, but no relationship or status transition authorised by Section 7's Revision Qualification Gate qualifies the submission as a revision of the target `KnowledgeItem`. No lifecycle event is appended and the existing item remains entirely unchanged. This is a structural qualification failure, distinct from `Structurally unresolvable` (the reference does not resolve at all) and from any evidential-sufficiency judgment (Section 7 expressly disclaims a numerical materiality threshold) — it must never be described as, or treated as, evidential insufficiency.
- **Restoration barred by non-resolution.** Restoration was attempted against a `KnowledgeItem` whose originally-cited evidence reference does not resolve through `MemoryRetrieval` (Section 9); the item remains retired. This outcome does not assert, and must not be implemented to assert, that the non-resolution was caused specifically by permanent owner erasure, per Section 9's own disclosed limitation.

**There is no separate "evidential insufficiency" rejection outcome for revision, and this document does not reverse that decision.** Unlike Unit 6's own promotion gate, which governs whether a candidate becomes a `KnowledgeItem` at all, a *qualifying* revision governs an already-promoted item and is never rejected for reaching an unchanged classification — it is `Applied` (Section 7's No-Change Revision Rule). `Not a qualifying revision` is a separate, structural gate failure (Section 7's Revision Qualification Gate), not an evidential-sufficiency judgment, and the two must not be conflated.

**Auditability.** Where applicable to the outcome produced, a lifecycle operation's result and its corresponding `history` event must be capable of honestly disclosing: the target `KnowledgeId`; the operation type (revision, supersession-response, retirement, or restoration); the expected lifecycle sequence value the caller supplied; the item's actual current lifecycle sequence value, where the outcome is a stale-version conflict; the evidence reference(s) involved; the qualifying relationship type or status transition relied upon, where the outcome is `Applied` for a revision or restoration; the disclosed basis; the prior status or classification; the resulting status or classification, where applied; the event's `occurredAt`; and, where rejected, the specific reason among this section's six outcomes. This document does not prescribe a database schema, serialization format, or concrete result type beyond fixing that these fields and these six outcomes must be distinguishable; the persistence mechanism by which any of them is eventually stored is outside this document's scope.

## 16. Explicit Exclusions

## 17. Binding Implementation Requirements

A future Unit 7 implementation **SHALL**: represent `history` as a single, ordered, append-only sequence over the closed three-variant lifecycle-event contract (Section 5); never mutate a previously appended event or a previously returned `KnowledgeItem` value (Section 6); recognise a submission as a revision only where it satisfies Section 7's Revision Qualification Gate (an `AMENDS`, `SUPERSEDES`, `CONTRADICTS`, or `DISPUTES` relationship between the new and already-cited evidence, or a status transition on the already-cited evidence itself), and otherwise treat it as `NotQualifyingRevision`, never as an ordinary revision merely by `KnowledgeId` association (Section 7); never treat a qualifying revision that reaches an unchanged classification as a rejection (Section 7's No-Change Revision Rule); represent supersession as an ordinary revision using the existing `KnowledgePromotion` shape, triggered only by a `SUPERSEDES` relationship or a `SUPERSEDED` status transition (Section 7); never write any status or relationship into Memory Core (Section 7); reject repeated retirement of an already-retired item and repeated restoration of an already-active item (Sections 8, 9); require restoration to satisfy Section 9's Restoration Qualification Rule, and never permit restoration where the originally-cited evidence reference fails to resolve through `MemoryRetrieval`, without asserting that this non-resolution proves permanent owner erasure specifically (Section 9); enforce a monotonic, per-item, compare-and-append lifecycle sequence as the sole ordering and conflict-detection mechanism, uniformly across revision, supersession, retirement, and restoration, guaranteeing that a retried or concurrently duplicated operation can never apply twice against the same prior sequence value and instead surfaces as a stale-version conflict, without requiring an operation identifier (Section 10); treat that sequence, not any independent field, as the lifecycle version, and never as part of `KnowledgeItem` identity (Section 11); never require or depend on timestamp equality or ordering for correctness or for retry-safety (Section 12); treat revision, supersession-response, retirement, and restoration as non-Permission-Engine-gated internal computation, while never conflating any future Owner-request input with Permission Engine authorisation (Section 13); and express exactly the six outcomes Section 15 names, never fewer, never more, without invented additional categories.
